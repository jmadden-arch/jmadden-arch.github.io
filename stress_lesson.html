Types of Stress

Eustress = Positive

Positive Personal Stressors

- Starting a new job voluntarily
- Receiving a promotion or raise at work
- Going on a holiday or vacation
- Moving in to a new home
- Learning something new
- Retiring
- Buying a new home
- Having a child
  • Positive
  • Energizes
  • Generally Short-Term
  • Can improve performance
  • Believed within coping mechanisms (i.e. something we can handle)

Distress = Negative

Negative Personal Stressors

- Death of a significant person
- Financial challenges
- Major personal injury or illness
- Unemployment
- Legal problems
- Being abused or neglected
- Change in health or behavior of a family member
- Being fired at work
  • Feels unpleasant
  • Depletes Energy
  • Short or Long Term
  • Decreases overall performance
  • Outside coping mechanisms (i.e. something we cannot handle)

How does the body respond to Stress?

Tap each stage to preview it.

General Adaptation Syndrome (GAS):

a series of body changes that result from stress

FIRST Stage:

ALARM STAGE- body gets ready for action. Your brain perceives a situation as threatening or uncomfortable and reacts.

Adrenaline: a chemical released from the brain that prepares our body to deal with STRESS and is responsible for the following:
heart rate & blood pressure
Digestion
Respiration & sweat
Mental activity
Pupils dilate

SECOND Stage

RESISTANCE STAGE- Body attempts to regain internal balance

adrenaline
heart rate, blood pressure
respiration
sweating stops
muscles relax
***The body must RESIST to AVOID the last stage***

FINAL stage

Exhaustion Stage- This stage is the result of prolonged or chronic stress. Struggling with stress for long periods can drain your physical, emotional, and mental resources to the point where your body no longer has strength to fight stress

COPING with STRESS

Definition of ‘Cope’
to deal with and attempt to overcome problems and difficulties

Positive Examples:

  • Listening to music
  • Playing with a pet
  • Laughing or crying
  • Taking a bath or shower
  • Writing, painting, or doing other creative activities
  • Exercising or getting outdoors to enjoy nature
  • Discussing situations with a close friend
  • Deep breathing, meditation, or muscle relaxation
  • Create an action plan or ‘to-do’ list
  • Seeking counseling if you continue to struggle with stress

Negative Examples:

  • Criticizing yourself (negative self-talk)
  • Becoming aggressive or violent (hitting someone, throwing or kicking something)
  • Eating too much or too little or drinking a lot of coffee
  • Smoking or chewing tobacco
  • Drinking alcohol
  • Yelling at family or friends
  • Taking a recreational drug, or misusing prescription medicine
  • Avoiding friends and family
