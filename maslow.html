1. What motivates you?8 minutes

Intrinsic motivation comes from inside you: you do something because you enjoy it or it matters to you. Extrinsic motivation comes from outside: you do something for a reward, a grade, or to avoid a consequence.

Think of one thing you did this week. Was it intrinsic or extrinsic? Explain.

Which type do you primarily have? Which type do you think leads to more success, and why?

2. Where motivation comes from5 minutes

Your motivation to act comes from your needs. You need to eat to survive, brush your teeth to keep them healthy, and work to earn money for food and rent. Some needs are biological, like drinking water when you are thirsty. Others are emotional, like wanting to do well on an exam.

Your needs change over your lifetime. Your thoughts, beliefs, feelings, environment, culture, and relationships all affect what you need at a given moment.

When needs are not met, you may feel like something is missing. You then try to fill that gap.

Name one need you have met today and one you are still working on.

3. Maslow's Hierarchy10 minutes

Maslow's hierarchy says people are motivated to meet basic needs first before moving on to higher ones. For humans to reach their total potential, these needs must be met.

Tap each level to learn more. Start at the bottom.

Levels explored: 0 of 5

Select a level above.

Deficiency needs The first four levels come from things we are lacking. They can only be met by outside sources: the environment, people, or things around us.

Growth need Self-actualization does not fill a lack. It gives you room to grow. It is always intrinsically motivated, because you do it out of pure enjoyment and a desire to grow.

Self-actualization is the top level. It means realizing your potential, self-fulfillment, seeking personal growth, and having peak experiences.

Which level do you think is hardest to reach, and why? What is one thing that could be self-actualization for you?

4. Practice with real scenarios10 minutes

Match each scenario to the need being met (or not met). Choose an answer and check it right away. If you miss one, read the hint and try again.

5. Wrap-up7 minutes

Summarize Maslow's hierarchy in your own words. Include at least 3 of the 5 levels.

Think of a time a need at a lower level (like sleep or feeling safe) made it hard to focus on something higher (like school). What happened?
