What cancer actually is

Learning target: explain the difference between regulated cell division and cancerous growth.

Normal

Division with brakes

Healthy cells divide only when the body needs replacements — to heal a cut, replace skin, grow. When a cell is damaged or old, it self-destructs on schedule. Growth and death stay in balance, so the tissue keeps its shape.

Cancerous

Division with the brakes cut

A mutation damages the instructions that tell a cell when to stop dividing and when to die. That one cell keeps copying itself, and every copy carries the same broken instructions. The pile of extra cells becomes a tumor.

Benign or malignant

Learning target: distinguish benign and malignant tumor pathology. Not every tumor is cancer — the difference is whether it can leave home.

Benign

Encapsulated — wrapped in a border that keeps it contained. It stays in one place, grows slowly, and does not invade nearby tissue. It can still cause problems by pressing on something important, but it will not spread.

Malignant

Invasive — it pushes fingers of tissue into whatever is next to it, and it can break off and travel through blood or lymph to start new tumors elsewhere. That travel is called metastasis, and it is what makes cancer dangerous.

The ABCDE check

B — BorderEdges are ragged, notched, or blurry instead of smooth.

Finding it early

Learning target: match detection methods to what they're good at. Early detection is the single biggest factor in survival.

MRI

Strong magnets and radio waves build detailed pictures of soft tissue. No radiation, but loud, slow, and expensive.

Ultrasound

Sound waves bounce off tissue to make an image. No radiation, quick, and safe in pregnancy. Good for telling a fluid-filled cyst from a solid mass.

CT scan

A rotating X-ray builds cross-sections of the whole body. Fast and excellent for finding whether cancer has spread — but it does use radiation.

Biopsy

A sample of the actual tissue is removed and examined under a microscope. The only test that can confirm cancer for certain.

Self-exam

You checking your own body on a schedule. Free, requires no appointment, and finds the majority of testicular cancers.

Mammogram

A low-dose X-ray of breast tissue that can spot a tumor years before anyone could feel it. The reason breast cancer death rates have been falling.

6

Treatment choices

Learning target: evaluate why a care team picks surgery, radiation, chemotherapy, or a combination.

Surgery

Cut the tumor out. Best when the cancer is in one place and reachable. Fast and complete for localized masses, but it can't reach cells that have already scattered.

Radiation

Focused energy aimed at one spot damages the DNA inside cancer cells so they can't replicate. Often used to shrink a tumor before surgery or to kill cells left behind after.

Chemotherapy

Drugs that travel through the bloodstream and kill fast-growing cells everywhere. That's why it works on cancer that has spread — and why it also hits hair, stomach lining, and blood cells, causing side effects.
