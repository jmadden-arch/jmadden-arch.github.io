Addiction & The Brain

From Abuse to Addiction

Nobody plans to become addicted. For many, it happens without realizing it. As drug use continues, the brain and body adapt, shifting from “wanting” the drug for fun to “needing” it to function.

Did you know?

Addiction isn’t about willpower. It is a chronic disease that affects the brain’s reward circuitry, altering how you process pleasure and stress.

The Downward Spiral: 4 Stages

Stage I

Experimentation and Desired Effect

Begins for fun, curiosity, or peer pressure. Drug use doesn’t interfere with hobbies or school yet.

Stage II

Tolerance

Tolerance begins. You need more for the same high. Drugs start to interfere with work/family.

Stage III

Dependence

Drugs are now priority #1. Withdrawal occurs (depression, anger, physical sickness).

Stage IV

Addiction

Total loss of control. Using to not feel sick rather than to feel high. Thoughts of suicide.

Uncovering the Two Forms of Addiction

The more you learn about addictions and how to stop them, the better you can live a safe and healthy life. Addiction manifests in two primary ways that often overlap.

Psychological Addiction

This is an emotional dependence on the feeling a drug or action provides. Your desire makes you feel that you can’t live without it.

Example

Cravings for the sensations of marijuana may lead you to adjust your whole life around getting high.

Physiological Addiction

Goes beyond mental cravings—your body has a marked physical need for the substance. Heroin, cocaine, nicotine, and alcohol are highly addictive in this way.

1.
Tolerance

The need for increasing doses to get the same high.

2.
Withdrawal

Sweating, tremors, and tension that appear when drug use stops.

Breaking the Myth: Overlap

Many experts believe these aren’t mutually exclusive. One form of addiction often brings on the other. Just because typical signs like tolerance don’t accompany every substance does not mean a person is not physically addicted. Once any addiction is formed, it is extremely harmful.
