Part 1: What is resting heart rate?

Your resting heart rate is how many times your heart beats per minute while your body is at rest.

  • A lower RHR generally means a more efficient heart and better cardiovascular fitness.
  • A normal RHR for most adults is about 60 to 100 beats per minute (Mayo Clinic).
  • Factors that influence heart rate: age, fitness level, smoking, medication, emotions, and body position when it is taken.
  • An unusually high or low RHR can point to an underlying health concern. Warning signs include fainting, dizziness, and shortness of breath. If you notice these, talk to a doctor.

Source: Mayo Clinic: What's a normal resting heart rate?

Part 3: Find your target heart rate zone

The Karvonen formula uses your resting heart rate to build a personal zone. (The heart rate monitor zone used in class is 145 to 185 bpm.)

  • Step 1: 220 − age − RHR = C
  • Step 2: C × 0.60 = A and C × 0.85 = B
  • Step 3: A + RHR = bottom of your zone; B + RHR = top of your zone

Worked example (age 18, RHR 52):

Step 1: 220 − 18 − 52 = 150
Step 2: 150 × 0.60 = 90 (A)
        150 × 0.85 = 127.5 (B)
Step 3: 90 + 52 = 142 (bottom)
        127.5 + 52 = 179.5 (top)
